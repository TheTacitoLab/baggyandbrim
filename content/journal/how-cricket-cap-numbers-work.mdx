A cap is a small thing to keep records on. A number changes that. Once a club starts numbering its caps, the cap stops being a piece of kit and becomes a line in the club's history, with a person attached to it.

## What a cap number is

A cap number is the position a player holds in the order they were first capped by the club. The first player capped is number one. The next is number two. The sequence runs from there and does not reset.

It is a record of belonging rather than a size or a batch. Two players can wear the same colours and the same crest and still carry different numbers, because the number is about them, not about the cap.

## How clubs number their caps

Most clubs number in order of debut. A player is given the next number in the sequence when they first play for the side the club has decided to cap, usually the first eleven.

Some clubs keep separate sequences for different teams, or for the men's and women's sides. Others number by decade for a commemorative run. There is no single correct system, only the one your club agrees to keep. What matters is that it is written down somewhere other than one person's memory.

## Where the number goes

The number is small, so it tends to sit somewhere quiet. Inside the cap, on the internal label or the sweatband, is the most common place. It can also go on the rear panel, where it reads as a detail rather than a decoration.

You can put a name and a year alongside it. A number places the player in the club. A name makes the cap theirs. A [custom baggy cap](/baggy-cricket-caps) with all three inside is a different object to a cap with a crest and nothing else.

## Starting a numbering system from scratch

Plenty of clubs have never numbered their caps and decide to start. The honest version is that it is a job. You work backwards through whatever records exist, honours boards, old fixture cards, scorebooks, and you build the sequence as far back as the evidence goes.

<KeyPoints items="Start with the current first team and number forwards from today.|Work backwards through the honours board as far as the records reach.|Write the sequence down and give one person the job of keeping it.|Agree what counts as a cap before you start, not after." />

Clubs that do this rarely regret it, even when the early numbers are uncertain. A sequence that starts today is still worth keeping, and it can be extended backwards later as more is found.

## Numbers and presentation caps

A number does its clearest work on a cap that is handed over. A [presentation cap](/cricket-presentation-caps) for a debut, a milestone or a life membership is the natural moment to record the number, because the cap is going to be kept and looked at rather than worn into the ground.
